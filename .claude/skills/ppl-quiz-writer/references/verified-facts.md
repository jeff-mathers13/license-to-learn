# Verified facts log

Everything below was confirmed via web search against official or reputable sources during past sessions writing questions for this app. Check here before searching again — if it's not here, it hasn't been verified yet.

## CARs (Canadian Aviation Regulations)

- **602.11** — Prohibits takeoff with frost, ice, or snow adhering to critical surfaces (wings, control surfaces, propellers, stabilizers). Known as the "Clean Aircraft Concept."
- **602.14** — Minimum heights: 1,000 ft above the highest obstacle within 2,000 ft horizontal radius over a built-up/congested area (602.14(2)(a)); 500 ft from any person/vessel/vehicle/structure over non-congested areas (602.14(2)(b)).
- **602.19** — Right-of-way rules. Priority order (highest to lowest): balloons > gliders > airships > power-driven heavier-than-air aircraft. Converging same-altitude same-category: aircraft with the other on its right has right of way. Head-on: both alter right. Overtaking: overtaking aircraft alters right.
- **602.20** — Aircraft on the water: head-on approach, both alter course to the right (mirrors the air rule).
- **602.24** — Formation flight requires prior arrangement between pilots-in-command.
- **602.27** — Aerobatic manoeuvres prohibited below 2,000 ft AGL without a special flight operations certificate.
- **602.36** — Altimeter setting procedures (transition altitude concept).
- **602.60(1)(b)** — No requirement to carry charts/publications for day VFR flight.
- **602.61–602.63 area** — ELT requirements (general reference, not a specific number verified beyond general requirement).
- **602.88** — Fuel requirements: destination + 30 min reserve at normal cruise for day VFR; 45 min for night VFR.
- **602.96, 602.96(3)** — Standard circuit turns to the left unless otherwise indicated/established as right-hand circuits.
- **602.114** — Controlled airspace cloud clearance: 500 ft vertically, 1 mile horizontally.
- **602.115** — Uncontrolled airspace VFR visibility: at/above 1,000 ft AGL by day, 1 mile; below 1,000 ft AGL by day (non-helicopter), 2 miles, clear of cloud.
- **602.116** — VFR-OTT (over-the-top): minimum 5 miles flight visibility at cruising altitude.
- **602.117** — Special VFR: minimum 1 mile ground visibility (non-helicopter) when reported; must remain clear of cloud with visual reference to surface.
- **101.01** — Definition of "night": the period between the **end of evening civil twilight and the beginning of morning civil twilight** — NOT a fixed "half hour after sunset" (that was the pre-2003 definition) and NOT the same as the US FAR "1 hour" rule. Don't conflate these three different definitions across jurisdictions/eras.
- **202.26** — Certificate of Registration must be carried on board the aircraft at all times.
- **401.03** — Pilot must carry licence and medical certificate, produce on request while exercising privileges.
- **401.05** — Passenger-carrying currency: 5 takeoffs + 5 landings, same category/class, within preceding 6 months. Night passenger currency: additionally 5 night takeoffs + 5 night landings within preceding 6 months.
- **605.03** — Certificate of Airworthiness (flight authority) required.
- **605.95** — Aircraft journey log must be carried on board, except when the aircraft won't land/shut down anywhere other than its departure aerodrome.
- **SRAIT** (Search and Rescue Action Initiation Time) — if unspecified, search and rescue action generally begins 60 minutes after the last reported ETA.
- **ADIZ** — requires a flight plan or itinerary filed in advance, showing estimated time and point of entry.

## Frequencies / codes

- **ELT frequencies**: 121.5 MHz (local homing) and 406 MHz (satellite-monitored, primary distress frequency).
- **Transponder emergency codes**: 7500 = unlawful interference/hijacking; 7600 = radio communication failure; 7700 = general emergency. (Standard ICAO codes, same in Canada.)

## Charts & format conventions

- **VNC (VFR Navigation Chart)**: Lambert Conformal Conic projection — straight line ≈ great circle route.
- **VTA (VFR Terminal Area Chart)**: Transverse Mercator projection — straight line = rhumb line.
- **GFA (Graphical Area Forecast)**: two chart types — Clouds & Weather, and Icing/Turbulence/Freezing Level. Cloud groups written as coverage + tops/base in hundreds of feet ASL (e.g. "BKN 80/40" = broken, tops 8,000 ft, base 4,000 ft ASL). TCU/CB noted directly after the cloud group when convective cloud is present.
- **METAR/TAF (Canadian)**: visibility always in statute miles (not meters). Wind: dddss(Gggg)KT, VRB for variable, 00000KT for calm. Cloud groups: coverage + 3-digit height in hundreds of feet AGL (single height per layer — different from GFA's tops/base fraction). Temp/dewpoint: whole °C separated by "/", M-prefix for negative (e.g. M06 = -6°C). Altimeter: A#### = inches of Hg × 100 (A2992 = 29.92 in Hg). TAF validity period format DDHH/DDHH; BECMG marks a gradual forecast change.
- **Weather intensity prefixes** (METAR/TAF/GFA, standard ICAO): "-" light, no prefix moderate, "+" heavy, attached directly to the phenomenon code (e.g. "-SHRA").
- **Icing/turbulence intensity codes**: LGT/MOD/SEV. Icing types: RIME/MXD/CLR. MECH = mechanical turbulence (terrain/surface-induced).

## Other specific facts

- **100LL avgas** is dyed **blue** for identification (standard color-coding).
- **Density altitude approximation**: DA ≈ pressure altitude + 120 ft × (OAT − ISA temp at that PA). ISA temp = 15°C − 2°C per 1,000 ft.
- **Cloud base rule of thumb**: base height (ft) ≈ (temp − dewpoint spread in °C ÷ 2.5) × 1,000.
- **Standard pressure**: 29.92 in Hg = 1013.25 hPa.
- **Longitude/time**: 15° of longitude = 1 hour (360°/24h).
- **PSTAR vs PPAER**: PSTAR is a separate, narrower, lower-level exam (student permit / air regs) most students pass before starting PPAER prep — its content isn't a good source for "new" PPAER practice material since students have typically already mastered it. (Also: TP 11919, the PSTAR guide, carries an explicit Transport Canada reproduction license, but was judged not worth using here for the reason above.)
- **602.34** — Hemispheric (semi-circular) cruising altitude rule: for VFR flight above 3,000 ft AGL, magnetic track 000°–179° → odd thousands of feet + 500 ft; magnetic track 180°–359° → even thousands of feet + 500 ft. Uses magnetic track in Southern Domestic Airspace, true track in Northern Domestic Airspace.
- **401.05** entries above cover passenger currency; separately confirmed medical certificates: Transport Canada issues **4 categories**. Category 1 = commercial/ATPL (strictest); Category 3 = minimum for a PPL; Category 4 = recreational/student self-declaration (least strict); Category 2 exists for a different group (not pilots specifically — didn't fully verify, don't overstate this one in a question).
- **MEL (Minimum Equipment List)** — specifies equipment that may be inoperative for dispatch, and any conditions/limitations while it remains so. General aviation concept, not CAR-numbered in what's been verified here.
- **Mandatory Frequency (MF) areas** — pilots are expected to broadcast position/intentions at defined points (joining circuit, before taxiing, etc.) on the MF.

## Other specific facts (continued)

- **DME (Distance Measuring Equipment)** measures line-of-sight **slant range**, not pure ground distance — includes the aircraft's altitude component. Difference is small at typical en-route range, more noticeable close to or above the station.
- **Aircraft fire extinguishers**: clean agents (Halon, or modern alternatives like Halotron) are the recommended type for aircraft cabins — confirmed across multiple sources (SKYbrary, AVweb, HRD Aero Systems). Dry chemical (ABC) types are specifically discouraged because they leave corrosive residue and severely impair cockpit visibility. Minimum-rating specifics (e.g. 5B:C) seen in sources but not deeply verified — safe to state the Halon-vs-dry-chemical distinction, less safe to cite an exact rating number.
- **Ceiling** (aviation weather definition): height AGL of the lowest cloud layer reported as broken, overcast, or an obscuration — below 20,000 ft, per standard definition.
- **Tropopause**: boundary between the troposphere and stratosphere, where the standard ISA lapse rate essentially stops.
- **Clear air turbulence (CAT)**: turbulence in cloud-free air, commonly associated with the jet stream or strong wind shear aloft — distinct from convective or mountain-wave turbulence.
- **PARE spin recovery**: Power idle, Ailerons neutral, Rudder full opposite the direction of rotation (held until rotation stops), Elevator forward to break the stall — standard NACA-derived technique for most light aircraft absent a different manufacturer-specified procedure. Confirmed across CFI Notebook, SKYbrary, Boldmethod, and Wikipedia's PARE article. Related bonus facts: both wings are stalled in a spin (the inside/lower wing more deeply); aft CG makes recovery harder and can risk a flat spin; common inadvertent-spin scenarios include base-to-final turns and go-arounds with excessive nose-up trim.
- **Hydraulic lock** is specifically a **radial engine** issue (cylinders pointing downward allow oil to pool) — not applicable to the horizontally-opposed engines used in typical PPL trainers (C152/172, PA-28, etc.). Judged lower priority for this app despite being a real, verifiable topic, since it's not relevant to the aircraft most students are actually flying.
- **Winds aloft forecasts (FD) are referenced to true north**, same as METAR/TAF — confirmed across multiple sources (Wikipedia, NOAA Pilot's Guide to Aviation Weather Services, FlyingMag, ForeFlight support docs). This parallels the already-verified METAR/TAF-true vs. ATC/ATIS-magnetic distinction — one more data point in the same pattern.
- **Hydroplaning has three recognized types**: dynamic (high-speed, water film builds a wedge under the tire, needs ≥0.1" depth), viscous (needs only a very thin film, ~0.001", but requires a smooth/rubber-contaminated surface — can occur at much lower speed than dynamic), and reverted rubber (a locked/skidding wheel's friction melts the tire, boiling trapped water to steam). Confirmed across SKYbrary, Boldmethod, Gleim Aviation, and an NTSB reference document — highly consistent across sources.
- **Wheelbarrowing** is specifically excess weight/load on the nosewheel (from excess speed or forward pressure), reducing main-gear traction/steering and risking directional control loss — confirmed alongside the hydroplaning search (a Mastery Flight Training source explicitly warns against pushing the nose down and inducing wheelbarrowing while trying to avoid hydroplaning, confirming both the mechanism and that they're related-but-distinct hazards).
- **Runway numbering**: the runway number is the magnetic heading rounded to the nearest 10°, with the trailing zero dropped (Runway 27 ≈ 270° magnetic) — standard, high-confidence aviation convention, no additional verification needed.
- **Ice-induced roll upset**: an uncommanded rolling moment caused by ice disrupting airflow near the ailerons, sometimes causing unpowered control surfaces to self-deflect. Confirmed across SKYbrary and FAA AC 91-51A. Recovery is essentially the same as incipient stall recovery (reduce angle of attack, add power) and is the *opposite* of tailplane-stall recovery — misdiagnosing which one is happening and applying the wrong technique makes things worse, per SKYbrary.
- **Center of pressure movement**: on a typical cambered airfoil, CP generally moves *forward* as angle of attack increases toward the stall, then can shift rapidly aft near/at the stall itself — standard, well-established aerodynamics teaching.

- **AIP Supplement vs. NOTAM vs. Canada Flight Supplement (CFS)**: NOTAMs cover short-duration changes; AIP Supplements cover longer-duration (roughly 3+ months) temporary information or anything needing graphics/detail a plain-text NOTAM can't convey. The CFS is Canada's official aerodrome/airport directory (runway data, frequencies, navaids, services), published every 56 days on the AIRAC cycle, jointly civil/military, managed by NAV CANADA. Secondary Surveillance Radar requires the aircraft's transponder to reply to a ground interrogation, providing identity and (Mode C/S) altitude — richer data, but dependent on working equipment. Confirmed across SKYbrary, Wikipedia, and multiple radar-engineering references, all consistent.

CFPS (Collaborative Flight Planning Services) is the modern online portal that replaced the older AWWS system for flight planning and weather. Confirmed via NAV CANADA's own flight-planning and FIC pages.

- **CRFI (Canadian Runway Friction Index)**: reported as a decimal between 0 and 1, measured by a decelerometer test vehicle — lower values mean reduced braking friction (more slippery), longer stopping distances, and greater risk of crosswind drift. Confirmed across multiple sources including TC AIM references and a Transport-Canada-sourced Aerodrome Safety Circular framing.
- **Trowal (trough of warm air aloft)**: a distinctly Canadian meteorological term (developed by Canadian meteorologists, per academic literature) for the wedge of warm air lifted aloft during occlusion — the associated heaviest weather (precipitation, embedded thunderstorms, icing) is often centred on the trowal itself, offset from where the occluded front symbol is drawn on a surface map. Confirmed across NAV CANADA's own Aviation Meteorology Reference, SKYbrary, and academic sources (Monthly Weather Review), all consistent.

- **Chinook winds**: a foehn-type warm, dry downslope wind on the lee (eastern) side of the Rocky Mountains, most associated with Alberta. Confirmed across SKYbrary, IFR Magazine, and multiple academic sources. Can raise temperature dramatically within hours (sources cite up to 30+°F in an hour in extreme cases), drops relative humidity sharply, and is a real aviation hazard via turbulence and wind shear from the associated eddies/oscillations — distinctly relevant given Squamish/Western Canada context.
- **Cold fronts generally move faster than warm fronts** — standard, well-established meteorology fact, part of why cold-front passage tends to be abrupt and warm-front passage more gradual.
- **VOR cone of confusion**: standard, well-established concept — the roughly conical zone directly above a VOR station where bearing indications become erratic, a normal and expected effect of flying overhead rather than a fault.
- **Cabin heat via exhaust heat exchanger** carries a real, well-known carbon monoxide risk if the heat exchanger cracks or leaks — standard GA safety topic, high confidence, no additional verification needed beyond general aviation knowledge.
- **DME arc**: a navigation technique of flying a curved path at constant DME distance from a station, using the distance readout (not a bearing) to stay on the arc.
- **Mountain obscuration**: a forecast term for peaks/ridgelines being hidden by cloud, precipitation, or haze — distinct from a cloud ceiling/base report.
- **Sleep debt**: accumulates from repeated inadequate sleep over multiple days/weeks and isn't resolved by a single night's sleep — distinct framing from ordinary acute (short-term) fatigue.
- **METAR/TAF wind direction is referenced to TRUE north; ATC/ATIS spoken wind is referenced to MAGNETIC north.** Confirmed across multiple sources (pilotsyllabus.com, SKYbrary). The reason: runways are numbered magnetically, so ATC converts wind to magnetic to match how pilots think about it for takeoff/landing, while the written/coded report stays true. Genuinely useful, testable distinction.
- **CAVOK**: sources disagree on the literal letter expansion — some say "Ceiling And Visibility OK," others "Cloud And Visibility OK." Given the inconsistency, questions about CAVOK should test its functional meaning/criteria (visibility ≥10km, no cloud below 5,000 ft or the highest MSA, no CB/TCU, no significant weather) rather than asserting one specific expansion of the acronym. Confirmed: not used in the US, but used in Canada (TC AIM references it) and internationally per ICAO Annex 3.
- **Position lights AND anti-collision lights are two distinct required equipment items** per CARs equipment standards — not one combined requirement. (Paceline Pilot Training source, cross-referencing CAR equipment standards.)
